Banana Climbing / Apparel graphics

Climbing culture,
worn every day.

From climbing holds
to a T-shirt.

T-shirt graphics designed during my internship at Banana Climbing. The shapes and names of climbing holds become colourful line drawings, bringing a familiar part of the gym onto everyday clothing.

Context
Internship · Banana Climbing
My contribution
T-shirt graphic design
Shown here
Two layouts & worn garments
The graphic language ↓Two layouts ↓In use ↓

01 / The ideaA vocabulary from the wall.

Jug, undercut, crimp, pocket and sloper: the back graphic pairs climbing-hold shapes with their names. Loose outlines and lettering give the technical vocabulary a playful, hand-drawn character.

A small chest graphic keeps the front restrained. On the back, yellow, green and orange lines spread across the dark fabric, with white lettering linking the illustrations to Banana Climbing.

02 / The designTwo ways to arrange
the same elements.

The two design layouts use the same chest graphic and illustrated holds. The difference is on the back: one overlaps lettering and illustrations; the other gives each element more space.

03 / In useOff the artwork.
Onto the body.

Photographs of the worn T-shirts show how the two print scales work together: a compact mark on the chest, and a larger illustration across the back. The gym places the graphics alongside the climbing culture they draw from.
